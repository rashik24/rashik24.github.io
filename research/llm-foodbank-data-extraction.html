Research Project

LLM-Based Food Bank Data Extraction and Automation

Developing an AI-enabled pipeline that extracts and standardizes food assistance information from websites, PDFs, and other semi-structured sources for use in mapping, accessibility analysis, and operational decision-support systems.

Overview

Food assistance information is often distributed across food bank websites, partner-agency webpages, online directories, PDFs, and other semi-structured sources. Important operational information such as addresses, operating hours, service schedules, eligibility requirements, and service types may only be available as free text.

This project develops an automated data extraction pipeline using large language models and web-processing methods to convert these unstructured sources into standardized, machine-readable data.

Problem

Maintaining an accurate food assistance database is difficult because information is distributed across many independent sources and is frequently presented in inconsistent formats.

  • Agency information may be spread across multiple websites.
  • Operating hours are often written as free text.
  • Service schedules may vary by day, week, or month.
  • Eligibility information may be embedded in long descriptions.
  • Information may change without a standardized reporting process.
  • Manual extraction and verification are time-consuming.

Research Objective

The objective is to create a scalable workflow that identifies relevant information from heterogeneous online sources, extracts key attributes using large language models, validates and standardizes the extracted information, and converts the results into structured data for downstream applications.

The broader goal is to connect unstructured public information with operational analytics by producing reliable inputs for food-resource mapping, accessibility analysis, and decision-support systems.
